# Calculations

All values are computed on the watch from the configured coordinates; nothing is fetched from a
network and no example times are hard-coded. Results are validated in
[`validation-report.md`](validation-report.md) (generated by `tools/validate_calculations.py`).

## Prayer times, sunrise and sunset (`source/PrayerTimes.mc`)

* Algorithm: PrayTimes.org v2.3 by Hamid Zarrabi-Zadeh (solar declination and equation of time
  from the low-precision formulas of the U.S. Naval Observatory), one iteration.
* Sunrise/sunset: sun centre 0.833° below the horizon (refraction + semi-diameter), no
  elevation correction. Maghrib = sunset for every method.
* Dhuhr: solar transit, no added minutes.
* Asr: shadow factor 1 (standard: Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) or 2 (Hanafi).
* Fajr/Isha: twilight angles of the selected method; Umm al-Qura, Gulf and Qatar use
  Isha = Maghrib + 90 min (the 120 min used by Umm al-Qura during Ramadan is **not** applied).
* High latitudes: none / middle of the night / one-seventh of the night / angle-based
  (PrayTimes definitions, night = sunset → sunrise). With “none”, an undefined Fajr or Isha is
  shown as `--:--` and skipped by the countdown.
* Times are rounded to the nearest minute.

### Time zone, midnight and DST

* Every prayer is converted to an absolute UTC instant: `epochDay × 86400 + minutes × 60 − offset`.
  The countdown is `instant − now`, so it is independent of the displayed clock format.
* The “location day” is `floor((now + offset) / 86400)`. Today, tomorrow and the day after are
  computed and cached; the cache key is (day, offset), so the trigonometry runs once per day or
  when the offset or a setting changes, not every second.
* After Isha the next prayer is the following day’s Fajr, and the schedule row switches to the
  following day. Just after midnight the cache moves to the new day automatically.
* With “Watch time zone” the offset is `System.getClockTime().timeZoneOffset`, which includes
  daylight saving time, so DST changes are followed. Limitation: tomorrow’s times are computed
  with today’s offset; on the night of a DST change the pre-computed next-day Fajr can be off by
  one hour until local midnight, after which it is recomputed.
* With “Manual UTC offset” the prayer times are shown in the location’s time (useful when the
  watch is set to another time zone).

### Accuracy

Against the independent *adhan* implementation (different solar model) the difference is at
most 2 minutes for Djerba, Tunis, Makkah, Paris, New York, Karachi, Cairo and Singapore over
2026, and sunrise/sunset differ by less than 1 minute from NOAA (astral). At latitudes above
~48° in summer the high-latitude rules of different libraries are defined differently, so
Fajr/Isha can differ by up to ~15 minutes between apps; this is a convention, not an error.

Official timetables (e.g. the Tunisian Ministry of Religious Affairs, mosque calendars) often
add precautionary minutes (*tamkin*) to some prayers or use local adjustments. This watch face
shows the astronomical result for the selected method; it can differ by 1–5 minutes from a
printed local timetable. Users should follow their local mosque for the exact times.

## Hijri date (`source/HijriCalendar.mc`)

* Tabular Islamic calendar, civil epoch: 1 Muharram 1 AH = JDN 1948440 (Friday 16 July 622
  Julian); leap years 2, 5, 7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 21, 24, 26, 29 of each 30-year cycle; odd months
  30 days, even months 29 days (Dhu al-Hijjah 30 days in leap years).
* Identical to `convertdate.islamic` on all 46 752 days from 1950 to 2077.
* Compared with the Umm al-Qura calendar it is the same day about 36 % of the time and usually
  one day behind (57 %); see the validation report. **Neither calendar is guaranteed to match
  local moon sighting.** The “Hijri Date Correction” (−2…+2 days) shifts the day number before
  the conversion, so month and year transitions stay consistent; it does not affect the
  Gregorian date or the prayer times.
* The Hijri date changes at local midnight together with the Gregorian date (the traditional
  day starts at Maghrib).

## Gregorian date (`source/CivilDate.mc`)

Derived from the same epoch day as the Hijri date (Howard Hinnant’s `civil_from_days`), in the
watch’s time zone, so both dates always change at the same instant.
